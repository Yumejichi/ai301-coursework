# Voice guide: how I talk upstream

## Who I am in threads

I am an AI301 student practicing open-source contribution in Path Review.
I investigate an issue and share steps, evidence, and what I learned.
Readers can expect direct wording and clear limits on what I know.

## Rules I write by

### Rule: Promise investigation, not a fix

Say what I will test and promise findings, not a fix or deadline.

- Wrong: "I'll fix this by tomorrow."
- Right: "I'll test whether clicking Save twice creates duplicate entries and post my findings."

### Rule: Name the behavior

Describe the specific trigger and result instead of a generic claim.

- Wrong: "I'll look into this issue."
- Right: "I'll investigate the duplicate entries reported after clicking Save twice."

### Rule: Match certainty to evidence

Separate observations from guesses; limit cannot-reproduce conclusions to my test.

- Wrong: "There is no bug."
- Right: "I could not reproduce duplicate entries with the setup and steps below; my output shows one entry."

### Rule: Share my own proof

Report my own test and evidence, even when another student tested the same issue.

- Wrong: "Same as above, can confirm."
- Right: "My test also produced two entries; my environment, steps, and output are below."

### Rule: Follow disclosure requirements

Disclose AI assistance when required and describe its role accurately.

- Wrong: "I wrote this entirely myself." (when AI helped draft it)
- Right: "I used AI to help draft this report and checked its claims against my test output." (only after checking)

## Things I never post

- Promises of a fix or completion date.
- Claims that I ran a test before I actually ran it.
- Guesses presented as confirmed causes.
- Another person's reproduction presented as my own.
- Blame or insults toward maintainers or reporters.
